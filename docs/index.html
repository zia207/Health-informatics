Welcome to Health Informatics in Python — a tutorial series for health informatics professionals, clinical data scientists, and graduate students who want to work with health data as it actually exists: coded, standardized, exchanged, governed, and reused.

Health informatics is the discipline concerned with how health information is captured, structured, stored, exchanged, and used. This series starts with that infrastructure — EHRs, terminologies, HL7/FHIR, OMOP — and then applies Python to privacy, clinical text, decision support, claims, warehousing, bulk data, and emerging AI.

Modules: 4 parts · 16 notebooks
Level: Beginner through advanced · healthcare or public-health background helpful
Language: Python 3.10+ · Jupyter-compatible
Dataset: Synthetic throughout · no real patients
Author: Dr. Zia U. Ahmed · Upatta Analytics

Why Health Informatics in Python?

Analytics is only as good as the data infrastructure feeding it. This series focuses on the informatics layer that most analytics courses skip:

  1. Representation first — ICD-10, SNOMED CT, LOINC, CPT, and FHIR resources are treated as data types, not footnotes.
  2. Systems that talk to each other — ingestion, ETL, OMOP CDM, data quality, and bulk FHIR are the path from a hospital system to an analysis-ready table.
  3. Use with care — de-identification, clinical NLP, decision support, claims, and warehousing are shown as applied informatics, not isolated algorithms.
